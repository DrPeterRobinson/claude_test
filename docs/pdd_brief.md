
# Project definition document

## What is the project definition document?

The Project Definition Document (PDD) is a the first assessed element of your honours stage project.  Its purpose is to provide a solid starting point from which you can work.  The PDD describes the project in much more detail than the original brief title and summary, which is especially important if you are making changes.  You are expected to agree the scope and detail with your supervisor during your weekly meetings.  *It is the project you define in the PDD that you will be undertaking, so it's worth getting it right*.

The PDD provides evidence that you are spending time thinking, analysing and planning before pressing ahead with the development part of the project.  You might like to think of the PDD as similar to a research proposal, or a case for support from a sponsor.  If you were doing the project on a commercial basis, it could be used to convince people with money that you know what you are doing and can be trusted to deliver.  
  
A good PDD will take significant thought and planning, but will leave you in a sound position to make progress, with clear goals and a route map.  

> ### A note on timing
> You'll be creating the PDD during the first few weeks of trimester 1.  This doesn't mean you shouldn't be working on other parts of your project at the same time.  We'll talk about the detail of this in the lectures.
> 

## What's in the PDD?

The PDD will summarise the reasons for doing the project and give detailed primary and secondary aims.  It will include a set of intended deliverables, a time plan and a list of tasks.  It will feature a clear statement of proposed methodology, risk analysis, constraints and resource requirements.  It will consider any potential ethical or legal issues which might arise from the project's development or implementation.  

The PDD is a stand-alone document and makes up part of the portfolio of evidence which will be used to assess your project.  It will not be part of the final report document, though you may refer to parts of it and will certainly reflect on the task plan, aims and objectives when you come to write up your project.  The PDD does not need to include a significant literature review section you will be doing background research all through the project, and as part of your interim review you will creat a review summarising how your research and the literature inform your design and decision making.  The module lectures will provide detailed support about PDD development and content.

You are not expected to map out every detail of the project and every little task.  Unless you have an unusually clearly defined project, things will change over the course of the module.  You will write about this in the reflective section of your final report.  Nevertheless, the information in the PDD should generally attempt to answer the following questions as clearly as possible:

1. Why is the project being undertaken?
What is the background, and what is the rationale for doing it (that is, why is this project worth doing, not why are you as a person doing this project)?  What are the expected benefits that a successful project will deliver, and to whom?

2. What will the project deliver?
The scope of the project must be defined.  What are the primary objectives, and how will you know if they are complete?  Are there secondary objectives, and if so what are they?  How will success of the project as a whole be judged?  Bear in mind the appropriate programme competency.

3. How will the project be delivered?
What methodology will you use to deliver the project?  What tools will you use?  Will you conduct any experiments or surveys?  If so, how will these be managed, and how will the results be evaluated and used?  At the end of the project, how will you assess whether the methodology and tools were appropriate and successful?

4. What is the timescale for the project?
You have a clear end date, but how will you use the time you have available?  What are the major tasks, and how do they depend on each other?  How will you divide your time?

5. What risks and constraints might affect the completion of your project?
Are there any factors which could cause your project to fail?  What can you do to avoid them or deal with the consequences?  Are there parts which you already know are going to be difficult?  Do you have a free rein over the technologies and techniques you can employ, or are there constraints which mean you must use specific things? 

6. What are ethical and legal issues involved in conducting the project?
Will there be ethical concerns either in developing and conducting the project?  Are there any legal consideration (for example GDPR)?  Note that the PDD does not need to consider ethical issues resulting from the implementation of the project after it is complete - these will be considered elsewhere.

7. What are the commercial factors of the project?
How much will it cost?  How could it be commercially exploited?  What is the market for it?  Is there any competition?

8. How will you evaluate the project at the end?
Determining the success or otherwise of your project is not simply a question of saying 'I think it went well'.  You will need to specify in advance the criteria you are going to use to judge it, and the methods by which you will do so.  You have to write the exam questions before you take the test!

## What's NOT in the PDD?

The PDD sets out in general what you will do in the project, and the stages you will go through to complete it.  It should not contain all the details of how you will execute every last bit.  For example, it is likely that an early task in your project will be to produce a clear specification of what else needs to be done.  Another task following this might be to consider different technologies for achieving the aims, and to choose carefully which is most appropriate.  Unless there is some specific reason why your project must use a given technology (which is a _constraint_), then you don't put that in the PDD, because you haven't done the work to decide what the best choice is.  For example, suppose your project is to build a new social media network.  The PDD *won't* specify that it will be written in C# using a firebase back end (or any other particular technology), because that's a decision you make after analysis, not up front.  _Deciding on the tools and technology is part of the project itself, not the PDD (though you may choose to schedule this task at the same time as you are writing the PDD)._

## Use of AI and large language models

Your PDD (and everything else you submit) must be your own work.  This does not mean that you cannot use AI as a tool to assist you, but you must be careful how.  The University has a policy on the student use of AI, which you can find at https://www.hull.ac.uk/asset-library/docs/student-use-of-ai.pdf .  You are strongly advised to read and understand it (it's not very long).  The following guidance is based on the policy in the context of this assignment, and overrides it in some regards:

> You **may not** use AI to: 
> 
> - Paraphrase someone else's work without proper citation 
> - Create images, video or code without acknowledging the source 
> - Contribute to a viva or exam that is invigilated 
> - Perform research tasks such as generating data or acting as a source of factual information, without acknowledging the source 
>

Appropriate use of AI will be discussed further in the lectures for this module.  An important part of your final report will be a mandatory section reporting and reflecting upon your use of AI systems throughout your project.  There is no penalty for declaring appropriate AI use, but failure to disclose significant AI use may be considered academic misconduct.

## How will the PDD be evaluated?

The PDD will be assessed by your project supervisor and/or your project's second marker.  It will be assessed using the following elements:

| Element | Marks | Description |
| --- | :---: | --- |
**Project summary, background, aims and objectives** | 10 | The project must have clear aims.  Primary and secondary objectives should be defined and achievable (SMART).  The motivation and background for the project should be clearly explained.  The expected outcomes should be defined.  Any external constraints on the project should be clearly identified and explained.  
**Task plan** | 2 | A numbered list of the tasks required to complete the project will be well defined, at a level of detail consistent with the early stage of the process.
**Gantt chart** | 2 | A Gantt chart will clearly show these tasks, the timeline and the dependencies amongst them.  It should be realistic and detailed enough to allow project management.  You will be expected to use professional software to develop this (which will be provided).
**Risk assessment and mitigation plan** | 2 | The risks of the project will be carefully assessed: consideration will be given to the likelihood and severity of potential problems, and plans given for dealing with them if they happen.
**Identification of ethical and legal issues** | 2 | Any potential ethical issues involved with conducting the project (not with its deployment after completion) must be identified and consideration be given to addressing them.  If there are possible legal concerns (e.g. GDPR issues), these must be considered too.
**Commercial considerations** | 2 | The PDD should consider the project's cost, and what are the possibilities for commercialisation.
**Project methodology** | 2 | The PDD should explain how the project is going to be undertaken.  For a pure software development project, this might consider whether a particular development model is to be used; if the project includes research, it will describe how this will be performed, and how experimental results will be evaluated.  If it involves hardware design, this process must be described
**Evaluation plan** | 2 | The PDD must include details on how the methodology, design choices and final output will be objectively evaluated at the end of the project.  
**Document presentation** | 6 | The PDD is intended to be a professional document.  The standard of presentation, organisation, grammar and spelling is expected to be high.

## Feedback
You will receive feedback on your PDD from your supervisor, which should be borne in mind when progressing with the project.  Don't ignore your supervisor's advice!

## What needs to be submitted?
You must submit the PDD as a single document in PDF text format.  This should be based on the template which will be provided on canvas, though as long as you meet the content requirements you may make changes to its organisation and layout.  The PDD will be scanned via Turnitin.

You must also complete the associated declaration of AI usage, which is a separate Canvas assignment.

## Ethical approval (separate submission)
All projects must include an ethics evaluation, which in the first instance must be submitted via an online form in Canvas before the PDD can be submitted.  _This is separate to the consideration of ethics within the PDD_.  If as a result more detailed ethical approval is required, this will be conducted separately in conjunction with your project supervisor, and will not delay the PDD submission.
